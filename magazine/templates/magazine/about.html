Understanding the Data

The ISFDB organizes its records around two central concepts: titles and publications.

A title represents a work — a novel, short story, essay, or poem. A publication represents a specific edition or appearance of that work: a particular book printing, a magazine issue, or an ebook release. One title can have many publications. The same novel published fifty times has one title record and fifty publication records. When you browse a magazine issue you are looking at a publication; when you click through to a story you reach its title record, which lists every publication that story has appeared in.

The title search and advanced search on this site cover the ten content types in the left column — the works that readers seek out. Art credits, reviews, interviews, and editor records are part of the bibliographic record but are not included in search results. This is why the count returned by a broad title search (about 1.76 million) is lower than the total shown in the stats above (about 2.55 million).

Languages

Titles are recorded in their original language. The database covers works in 136 languages, though English makes up the large majority (about 1.93 million titles). You can narrow any title search to a specific language using the Language filter in the advanced title search.

"First published" dates

The "First published" year shown in search results is the year of the earliest publication of that title recorded in the ISFDB — not necessarily the actual publication date. For well-documented works these match, but older or less widely covered titles may show a later date if earlier editions haven't been catalogued yet.

About This Project

This web site is a hobby project that combines two things that I have enjoyed for most of my life: Science Fiction and Computer Programming. I started programming (in Fortran II) 60 years ago, and started reading Science Fiction more like 70 years ago.

Creating this site has also given me a much better understanding of how far AI has come since I first played with the early releases of ChatGPT. The code for this web site (more than 13,000 lines of Python and HTML) is completely AI generated, based on the instructions I've given to Claude Code in conversation with that system.

The data itself is from the Internet Speculative Fiction Database, which is a massive collection of bibliographic information entered by volunteers from around the world. You can read more about the database and the ongoing project that created it, and continues to maintain it, at this FAQ page on the project's web site.

The web site runs as a Django application. The code can be viewed here: https://github.com/ecurbsniktaw/isfdb-explorer
